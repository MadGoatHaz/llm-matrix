<div class="callout"><p><b>The 30-second version:</b> You don't need to be an engineer to pick a local model. Tell us how much video memory (VRAM) your GPU has and what you want to do with it, and we'll line up the <b>real setups the community is actually running</b> that fit your hardware — ranked for you, with a plain-English reason for each one. Everything is computed right here in your browser from the <a href="../matrix.html">Matrix</a> data. No sign-up, no server, no guesswork.</p></div>

## How to use it

1. **Pick your VRAM.** This is the only required answer — and the single biggest constraint on what you can run. A model has to fit inside your video memory, with a little headroom left for the context cache.
2. **Tell us what you need** *(optional).* Coding, chat, long documents, research… We match your use case against what each community setup was actually used for.
3. **Set a priority** *(optional).* Do you care most about speed, quality, or how much the model can remember? We rank for that and show you the tradeoff.

## Reading the results

Each card is a **real, working setup submitted by a community member** — not a theoretical recommendation. You'll see the model, the hardware it ran on, its speed, context, and precision, plus a one-line **"why this matched."** Follow any card into the <a href="../matrix.html">Matrix</a> for the full submission and the original forum post.

<div class="callout callout--warning"><p><b>Stay honest with yourself:</b> these are community-reported numbers, not lab benchmarks. A setup that fits your VRAM tier is a great starting point, but your exact GPU, driver, and model file will change the speed. When in doubt, start with the <b>smallest model that does the job</b> — it's faster, cooler, and leaves you room to grow.</p></div>
