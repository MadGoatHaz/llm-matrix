Find Your Model

Tell us what you have and what you need — we'll rank the community's real fits.

The 30-second version: You don't need to be an engineer to pick a local model. Tell us how much video memory (VRAM) your GPU has and what you want to do with it, and we'll line up the real setups the community is actually running that fit your hardware — ranked for you, with a plain-English reason for each one. Everything is computed right here in your browser from the Matrix data. No sign-up, no server, no guesswork.

How to use it

  1. Pick your VRAM. This is the only required answer — and the single biggest constraint on what you can run. A model has to fit inside your video memory, with a little headroom left for the context cache.
  2. Tell us what you need (optional). Coding, chat, long documents, research… We match your use case against what each community setup was actually used for.
  3. Set a priority (optional). Do you care most about speed, quality, or how much the model can remember? We rank for that and show you the tradeoff.

Reading the results

Each card is a real, working setup submitted by a community member — not a theoretical recommendation. You'll see the model, the hardware it ran on, its speed, context, and precision, plus a one-line "why this matched." Follow any card into the Matrix for the full submission and the original forum post.

Stay honest with yourself: these are community-reported numbers, not lab benchmarks. A setup that fits your VRAM tier is a great starting point, but your exact GPU, driver, and model file will change the speed. When in doubt, start with the smallest model that does the job — it's faster, cooler, and leaves you room to grow.

How the ranking works

Fit first. A result only appears if it runs in your VRAM tier (or a smaller one). Setups bigger than your card are never shown — they wouldn't fit.

Then your needs. Pick a use case and we match it against what each setup was actually used for. Pick a vendor and matching hardware ranks higher. Your priority (speed / quality / context) sets the main sort order.

Always honest. Every card is a real community submission with a plain-English "why this matched." The numbers are community-reported, not lab-tested — a starting point, not a guarantee.
